01 // MISSION BRIEF

CONTEXT

Reimagining test ride booking to eliminate customer drop-offs and bring rides home.

This mission covers the complete redesign of Ather Energy's online test ride booking system. The core intervention was the introduction of Home Test Rides (HTR) — a first-of-its-kind feature that brought the test ride experience directly to the customer's location, removing the friction of visiting an Experience Centre.

The existing booking flow presented a fragmented, multi-step journey that required users to locate nearby ECs, navigate an unintuitive calendar, and commit personal details before understanding availability — a sequence that drove high abandonment.

02 // PROBLEM STATEMENT

WHAT WAS BROKEN

Key usability hurdles preventing customers from completing their booking.

01
Complex Booking Flow
The existing booking sequence was lengthy and cognitively demanding. Users faced multiple decision points with insufficient guidance, leading to confusion and early abandonment before they could commit to a ride.
02
Ambiguous Location Data
Location information for Experience Centres was presented in a way that created confusion — distances were unclear, and the interface failed to surface the nearest available slot relative to the user's position.
03
Mandatory EC Visits
Requiring in-person visits to Experience Centres was a structural barrier. The absence of a home test ride option eliminated a major segment of potential customers from completing the flow.

04 // STRATEGY & RATIONALE

THE DECISIONS

How we tackled timing, location friction, and vehicle availability.

Decision 01

The Timing Problem

Testing Something We Couldn't See Clearly
Is the stepped flow actually better, or does it just feel better to us?
  • 1Moved location selection to the top of the sequence so each step felt deliberate, not rushed.
  • 2Stepped flow worked because it forced intentionality — users couldn't reach date selection without confirming location first.
  • 3Ran an A/B test comparing the stepped booking flow against the single-page version — the dropout we feared didn't materialise.
Decision 02

The Location Problem

Defending Two Decisions at Once
How do you hold your ground when stakeholders challenge your core assumptions?
  • 1Observed 40% abandonment rate attributable to Experience Centre location difficulty.
  • 2Defended Home Test Ride against cultural pushback — data showed serious buyers still got stuck finding an EC.
  • 3Used the same evidence to defend the stepped flow when stakeholders preferred a single long form.
Decision 03

The Inventory Problem

Designing Around a Physical Constraint
How do you build a booking system when you have 2–4 test ride vehicles per location?
  • 1Designed a chip-based date selector around real inventory limits — overbooking was an operational risk.
  • 2Chips load availability in real time based on the selected location, so users only see dates with a vehicle.
  • 3Surfaces the nearest available option instead of a calendar that invites distant future planning.
05 // ARCHITECTURE

THE FLOW

Restructuring the booking journey around Home Test Rides.

We restructured the entire booking journey — introducing the Home Test Ride branch as the primary path while preserving the Experience Centre route for users who preferred it.
