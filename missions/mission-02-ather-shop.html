01 // The Backstory

Context

Ather Shop worked fine on desktop, but almost everyone visited on their phone. The product team's analytics showed most of the traffic was on phones, so the brief was mobile first. And the mobile experience was rough: confusing navigation, no search, and product pages that weren't built for touch.

Our goal was simple: fix the mobile experience so people could find what they wanted, add items to their cart, and check out without getting frustrated.

03 // The Thinking

The Decisions

DECISION 01

A web project that became a mobile project

Are we solving a design problem or a strategy problem?
The Ather Shop was built for desktop users. Mobile was a responsive afterthought: the same content, scaled down. The product team's analytics showed most sessions came from phones, so the brief changed. Mobile became the primary surface and I designed from the phone up. Every later choice started from that.

DECISION 02

Search as a scale decision

When does a product need search?
Search wasn't on the roadmap because the original product range was small. As Ather expanded across scooter verticals, the catalogue grew to a point where browsing alone couldn't serve users who arrived with intent. The theme's built-in search wouldn't do. It only matched the exact product name, and most people don't know it. I designed a search that also works on product type, suggests products and shows results while you type, and looks like the rest of the shop. Shoppers who used search converted 2.25x more than those who didn't.
Search with suggestions and live results
DECISION 03

Buy Now for people who came for one thing

Why make people go through the cart for one product?
Most people came to the shop for one product. Only 2.5% of buyers checked out with more than one item in their cart. Add to cart and then the cart was extra steps for a single purchase, so I added a Buy Now button and designed the page so people could see what they were buying before they tapped it.

04 // The Build

Design Process

  1. STEP 01

    Research and problem discovery

    We analysed session recordings and funnel data to map where mobile users were abandoning. We interviewed Ather Shop users to understand how they look for products. Most arrived with a category in mind, not a specific product name.

  2. STEP 02

    Navigation and search

    I restructured the navigation for touch first use: clear category entry points, persistent breadcrumbs and a global search bar in the top bar. On listing pages I redesigned the filters as scrollable chips that work with one thumb.

  3. STEP 03

    Content and assets

    Many images and content blocks weren't made for mobile. I worked with the agency that owned the assets and with the content team, and fixed some of the assets myself where I had access.

  4. STEP 04

    Product page

    The product page got the biggest change: a sticky Buy Now bar, a swipeable image gallery, inline size guidance with a 'how to measure' expandable, and a clear discount display. Add to cart and Buy Now got different visual weight to reduce decision paralysis.

  5. STEP 05

    Phased release

    We released in phases. Search and navigation shipped first to set a data baseline, and the product page redesign followed, so metric changes could be tied to specific design changes.

05 // The Shift

Before & After

Before

The old mobile shop

  • No search bar: zero path for intent-driven product discovery
  • Category browse only: required navigating full product listings
  • Non-sticky CTA: "Buy Now" scrolled out of view on long PDPs
  • No size guide: users had to guess or leave to find guidance
  • Basic image gallery: no swipe support, arrows only
  • Filter chips required multiple taps: friction-heavy selections
After

The redesigned shop

  • Global search with category-scoped results and empty state guidance
  • Category tiles with product photography: visual-first navigation
  • Sticky bottom bar: price + "Add to cart" + "Buy now" always visible
  • Inline size chart with step-by-step measurement guide
  • Full-width swipeable image gallery: touch-native interaction
  • Horizontal scrolling filter chips: one-hand operation optimised
